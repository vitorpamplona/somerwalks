# Sidewalk Snow Rules: Faster, Simpler, More Accessible

Somerville Pedestrian & Transit Advisory Committee (PTAC) · Nov 3, 2026

Copy of the PTAC Claude Doc ("Proposal" tab). The live doc is the working version: https://claude.ai/artifact/7vG43e3w32sGB4efaCwyZE (the chart appears here as a table).

Dear Members of the City Council,

On behalf of the Somerville Pedestrian and Transit Advisory Committee (PTAC), thank you for your continued attention to walking and transit in our city. In March 2025, PTAC wrote to Mayor Ballantyne, with copies to the City Council, after finding ice on sidewalks up to 7 days after the snow that winter. That letter made 13 recommendations, from stricter rules for ice to one team leading snow clearing. Since then, the Council has required commercial buildings to clear their curb ramps (Ord. 2025-25). Most of the other recommendations need changes to the ordinances themselves, so this letter turns them into proposed text.

We respectfully ask the Council to amend Somerville's sidewalk snow ordinances so that sidewalks open the same day, the rules are simpler to follow and clear about every duty, and residents with disabilities are better protected at the places people most often get stuck: corners, bus stops and ice. Today the rules are hard to follow and harder to enforce. Complaints to 311 peak two to three days after the snow stops, long after the deadline, and nothing in the code makes sure a ticketed sidewalk is ever cleared. Below we explain why this matters and what we ask, followed by the proposed text. Attachment A explains each change, and Attachment B covers what the ordinance can't do alone.

## Why it matters

The deadline is measured in hours, but the danger lasts for days, and the people it strands are the ones who can least avoid it: wheelchair users, older residents, parents with strollers, and everyone walking to a bus.

## What we ask

### 1. Sidewalks open the same day

- **3 hours, not 6 daylight hours.** Bare pavement within 3 hours after the snow stops, by 10 p.m. (by 10 a.m. after overnight snow). In January, snow that stops at 1 p.m. is due at 4 p.m., not 9:34 the next morning. ([details](#sec-12-8d-when))
- **Clear while it snows.** Never more than 1 inch, 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. Nothing is due at night or during a snow emergency. ([details](#sec-12-8d-when))
- **Clear means clear.** Down to the pavement, whatever put the snow there: the sky, a plow, a roof or a neighbor. Ice that can't be removed is leveled and sanded, or it's a $300 ticket. ([details](#sec-12-8c-the-path-and-driveways))

### 2. Every ticket ends with a clear sidewalk

- **Follow-through.** Inspectors must ticket, and they check priority routes without waiting for a complaint. They re-inspect within 24 hours; then the City clears what's still blocked and bills a flat fee. ([details](#sec-12-8h-and-i-when-the-city-clears-and-bills))

### 3. Corners, bus stops and ramps open for everyone

- **A path a wheelchair can use.** At least 4 feet wide, driveway crossings included. ([details](#sec-12-8c-the-path-and-driveways))
- **Open corners and bus stops.** Every corner ramp, with an opening through the plow bank to the street; a 48-inch strip along the curb at every bus stop. ([details](#sec-12-8c-corners-crosswalks-bus-stops-hydrants-parking-and-icicles))
- **ADA standards in winter.** The 8-by-5-foot boarding area at bus stops, signal push buttons kept reachable, accessible parking cleared, and a 311 fast lane to the City's ADA Coordinator when someone is stuck. ([details](#new-sec-12-8a-city-bus-stops-ramps-and-accessible-parking))
- **Safe detours.** Every construction permit requires clearing; detours are signed, detectable by cane and kept clear. ([details](#sec-12-8g-construction-sites))

### 4. Simpler rules

- **One section to read.** What to clear, by when, and where snow can't go, all in Sec. 12-8; Sec. 12-9 folds into it. ([details](#sec-12-9-repealed-now-sec-12-8e-and-h-snow-into-streets-bike-lanes-and-bus-stops))
- **One name on every ticket.** The owner of record, whatever a lease says, found in seconds in City records. ([details](#sec-12-8a-and-b-purpose-definitions-and-who-is-responsible))
- **Two flat fines.** Read from City records, with no 1st/2nd/3rd offense ladder and no warnings to weigh. ([details](#sec-1-11-fines-inspections-and-follow-up))
- **Help instead of fines** for lower-income owners who live in a 1- to 3-unit home and are 65 or older or have a disability. ([details](#sec-12-8f-help-for-those-who-cant-shovel))

### 5. The City does its share

- **The same rule on its own property,** and a clock for the bus stops, ramps and accessible parking it clears. ([details](#new-sec-12-8a-city-bus-stops-ramps-and-accessible-parking))
- **One official to coordinate, and a winter plan** each fall, with a clearing map and free sand or de-icer. ([details](#new-sec-12-8b-coordination-winter-plan-and-reporting))
- **A season report** to the Council each May, with costs and recommended changes. ([details](#new-sec-12-8b-coordination-winter-plan-and-reporting))

## The proposed text

Every change we propose, written as ordinance text for the City's legislative counsel to put into final form: the sidewalk rule (Sec. 12-8), the City's bus stops, ramps and accessible parking (new Sec. 12-8A), coordination, the winter plan, notices and data (new Sec. 12-8B), and the related changes to Sec. 12-2, 12-9 (repealed and merged into 12-8), 12-18 and 1-11. Attachment A explains each change from today's text and who it affects.

**Sec. 12-8. Snow and ice on sidewalks.**

**Purpose.** Sidewalks, curb ramps, crosswalks and bus stops are part of the city's transportation network. This section keeps them passable in winter for everyone, including people who use wheelchairs, walkers, canes or strollers.

**(a) Definitions.** In this section and in Sections 12-8A, 12-8B, 12-2, 12-18 and 1-11:

(1) "Sidewalk" means the paved way between the property line and the curb, including driveway crossings.

(2) "Owner" means the owner of record in the City's assessing records; for a condominium, its association.

(3) "Storm" means any snow, sleet or freezing rain, however light. It ends when it stops falling in the city or, if a snow emergency is declared, when the snow emergency ends.

(4) "Priority routes" means the bus routes, squares and school walking routes named in the winter plan under Section 12-8B(b).

(5) "City property" means land or buildings the City owns, including schools, parks, libraries, fire stations and parking lots, and the bridges and Community Path segments the City maintains.

(6) "Clear" means free of snow and ice down to bare pavement, whatever the source: snowfall, roofs, plows, wind or a neighbor. Sand or de-icer on snow does not make it clear.

(7) "Bus stop" means the sidewalk and curb from a bus stop sign back 40 feet, and any shelter or bench there.

**(b) Who is responsible.** The owner is responsible, whatever a lease says, even if away or using a contractor.

**(c) What to clear.** The owner must clear, and keep clear:

(1) a continuous path along each sidewalk the property abuts, both sides of a corner lot included, at least 48 inches wide (or the whole sidewalk if narrower), connecting to the neighbors' paths and to curb ramps (the "clear path");

(2) each abutting curb ramp, with its landing and raised warning surface, and a 48-inch path from it through any plow bank to the street (the "crosswalk opening");

(3) a strip at least 48 inches wide along the curb of any bus stop in front of the property, connected to the clear path;

(4) 3 feet around any hydrant on its sidewalk;

(5) on a property with a parking lot, whenever the lot is cleared, its accessible parking spaces, their access aisles and the route to the entrance;

(6) icicles, and snow or ice hanging over the edge of a roof, awning or sign above a sidewalk; and

(7) where a driveway crosses the sidewalk, the whole driveway from the property line to the street, apron included.

Ice that cannot be removed must be leveled and covered with sand or de-icer. Until icicles and overhanging snow or ice are removed, the owner must keep the sidewalk below marked with a sign, cones or tape.

**(d) When.**

(1) No one is required to clear between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., or while a snow emergency is in effect.

(2) Subsection (c) is due within 3 hours after the storm ends, but no later than 10 p.m.; if it ends between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., it is due by 10 a.m.

(3) While snow is falling from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., nothing subsection (c) requires clearing may hold more than 1 inch of snow or slush.

**(e) Where snow and objects may not go.**

(1) No one, contractors included, may put snow or ice on a sidewalk or anywhere else subsection (c) requires clearing, on a crosswalk, bike lane, storm drain, push-button pole, or accessible parking space or aisle, within 3 feet of a hydrant, or on a street except as paragraph (2) allows.

(2) Snow or ice from private land, or from the sidewalk, curb ramp or driveway apron abutting it, may go on a street only if broken up and spread no more than 3 inches thick, between 8 a.m. and 7 p.m., above 40°F.

(3) Snow in the planted strip between the sidewalk and the curb, plow snow included, may be no higher than 3½ feet above the sidewalk.

(4) No object, including a trash or recycling bin, may stand anywhere subsection (c) requires clearing or in a bus stop. Before each collection, the owner must clear a spot for its bins at the curb that collectors can reach from the street.

**(f) Snow assistance.** The City must run a snow assistance program. An owner who lives in a 1- to 3-unit building, is 65 or older or has a disability, and earns less than a limit the City sets may register at any time. Registering by December 1 covers the whole winter, October 1 to May 1. The City, its volunteers or organizations it funds clear what subsection (c) requires for registered properties, at no charge to the owner, by the deadlines of subsection (d). A registered property is not ticketed under this section.

**(g) Construction.**

(1) Every permit to place a dumpster, obstruct or open a sidewalk, or build or demolish, includes this condition: clear and keep clear what subsection (c) requires, by the deadlines of subsection (d).

(2) A permit that closes a sidewalk must provide an accessible detour at least 48 inches wide, signed before the closure, edged with barriers a cane can detect, and kept clear like a sidewalk under subsections (c) and (d).

(3) After 3 tickets for the property while a permit is open, the City may suspend a dumpster, obstruction or sidewalk-opening permit until the sidewalk is clear and the tickets are paid.

**(h) Follow-up and City clearing.** The inspector re-inspects within 24 hours after each ticket. A sidewalk still not clear gets a new ticket, and the Commissioner of Public Works must clear it within the next 24 hours. The owner also pays a flat fee, set at the City's average cost, for each 25 feet of sidewalk and each curb ramp the City clears. An unpaid fee becomes a lien, split among the units of a condominium. The Commissioner may also remove snow or ice placed in violation of subsection (e) and charge the owner a fee the City sets to match its cost. Tickets, City clearing and removal do not bar each other.

**(i) Collections.** Unchanged.

**(j) Liability.** The duties in this section and in Sections 12-8A and 12-8B are enforced by the City. They create no private right of action and do not enlarge the liability of the City or of any owner beyond what state law provides.

**State law reference.** M.G.L. c. 40, §21(2)–(4) and §21D, replacing today's reference to c. 85, §5, which caps a city's fine at $50.

**Sec. 12-8A. City clearing of bus stops, curb ramps and accessible parking.**

The City must clear the following within 24 hours after the storm ends, 12 hours on priority routes, and again within 24 hours after its plows bury them:

(1) at the bus stops the winter plan under Section 12-8B(b) assigns to it, a boarding area 8 feet deep and 5 feet wide (or the whole sidewalk if narrower) with the gutter in front of it, any shelter or bench, and 3 feet around a shelter's open sides out to the curb;

(2) curb ramps and crosswalk openings that no private property abuts, pedestrian refuge islands, and openings through the snow ridges plows leave between bike lanes and crosswalks, and snow at pedestrian push buttons, which stay reachable from the cleared curb ramp; and

(3) accessible parking spaces on its streets and in its lots, including snow emergency lots.

On priority routes, the City clears a curb ramp or crosswalk opening within 12 hours after a ticket for it, without waiting for the re-inspection under Section 12-8(h). While a storm lasts, stops on routes that are running must be kept passable by 8 a.m. and by 4 p.m.

**Sec. 12-8B. Coordination, winter plan and reporting.**

**(a) Lead official.** The Mayor must name one official (the lead official) to coordinate the City's snow and ice program across departments and with owners, volunteers and outside agencies: the winter plan, public information, inspections and enforcement, data and the season report.

**(b) Winter plan.** By October 1 each year, the lead official must publish a draft winter plan for comment, and by November 1 the final plan. The City carries out the plan, which sets out:

(1) the priority routes, with how they were chosen and what changed;

(2) the clearing order, a target time for streets, and a map of who clears each bus stop;

(3) free sand or de-icer for residents at published locations, announced each November, and pre-treatment of bus stops, curb ramps and sidewalks along City property on priority routes before forecast ice;

(4) a clearing log of when each bus stop, curb ramp, accessible parking space and sidewalk along City property is cleared (by GPS where available), shown on a public map during and after each storm;

(5) the written agreements the City seeks with the MBTA, the Massachusetts Department of Transportation and the Department of Conservation and Recreation on who clears their bus stops, station entrances and sidewalks in the city, and by when, including how stops stay passable while buses run during a storm;

(6) a season guide sent each November to every household and business, with the rules, photographs or illustrations of good and bad clearing, how to report a missed sidewalk, and the help available; and

(7) a public sign-up for neighbors who adopt a curb ramp, a bus stop or a registered neighbor's sidewalk under Section 12-8(f).

**(c) Emergency notices.** The City must announce the start and end of each snow emergency, with the owners' deadline after the emergency (Section 12-8(d)(2)) as a clock time, by City Alert and its other public channels, in the languages of its snow rules.

**(d) 311.** A 311 request about an uncleared sidewalk must be closed with its outcome: clear on inspection, ticket, cleared by the City, registered property cleared by the City, sidewalk along City property, or not found. 311 must also offer a request type for a blocked curb ramp, crosswalk opening, bus stop or path that keeps a person with a disability from leaving home or reaching transit. These requests go to the City's ADA Coordinator, and the person who asked gets a response, with a time for clearing, within 12 hours.

**(e) Season report and data.** By May 1 each year, the lead official must report on the past winter season to the City Council. The report gives complaints, inspections, tickets, City clearings and liens, sidewalks along City property found not clear, and how long the City took to clear bus stops, curb ramps and sidewalks along City property. It also gives the season's costs of snow and ice clearing, including City crews, contractors, de-icer and the snow assistance program, and the fines and fees collected; and it recommends any changes to these ordinances or the City's snow regulations for the next winter. By the same date, the City must publish the season's records on its open data portal: inspections and tickets under Sections 12-2, 12-8 and 12-18(e), with date, time, street and ward but no house number; City clearing and liens under Section 12-8(h); the clearing log under Section 12-8B(b)(4); and each declared snow emergency with its start and end times.

**Sec. 12-2. Water run-off and washing cars.**

Title and subsection (b) unchanged; icicles move to Section 12-8(c)(6).

**(a) Water.** No building, awning or other projection may shed or discharge water onto a sidewalk, curb ramp or bus stop. This includes roofs, downspouts, sump pumps and appliances. Water must be piped under the sidewalk to the gutter, or onto the owner's own ground.

**Sec. 12-9. Shoveling snow on streets.**

Repealed. Its rule on snow in the street moves to Section 12-8(e)(2), and City removal to Section 12-8(h).

**Sec. 12-18. Obstacles in streets and sidewalks.** Retitled from "Obstacles in streets or sidewalks at night"; subsections (a) through (d) unchanged. Add:

**(e) Space savers.** No one may put a chair, cone or any other object in a street to hold a parking space. The City may remove and discard it at once. Each object is a separate offense.

**Sec. 1-11. Penalties and violations (amendments).**

**In subsection (a),** strike "except that snow removal violations shall be counted from October 1 through May 1 of the following year", which the flat fines below make unnecessary.

**In the table in subsection (b),** replace the Sec. 12-8 and 12-9 rows with one row for Sec. 12-2 and 12-8: $150 for a building of 1 to 3 units where at least one unit has the residential exemption; $300 for any other property, and for any property for ice not leveled and covered with sand or de-icer by its deadline, or while a construction permit under Section 12-8(g) is open. Enforcing personnel: DPW commissioner; inspectional services; traffic and parking. Rename the Sec. 12-18 row "Obstacles in streets and sidewalks" (fine unchanged).

**Add subsection (d), snow and ice tickets:**

(1) A ticket under Section 12-2 or 12-8 names the owner of the property the sidewalk abuts, the snow or ice came from, or the object stands in front of. If not handed over, it is mailed within 15 days to the tax-bill address, or to a condominium's trustees or managing agent.

(2) An inspector who finds a violation must issue a ticket.

(3) During and after each snowfall of 1 inch or more at Boston Logan Airport, and within 24 hours after each snow emergency's deadline, inspectors must check the priority routes, whether or not anyone complained.

(4) Tickets and notices include how to pay, appeal and get help, in the languages in which the City publishes its snow rules.

(5) Fines and clearing charges under Section 12-8 must fund the snow assistance program and City clearing of curb ramps and bus stops, subject to appropriation.

(6) If 3 or more Section 12-8 tickets are issued between October 1 and May 1 for a property where a business holds a City license or permit, the issuing authority may suspend or refuse to renew it, after notice and a hearing.

## Attachment A: The changes, section by section

Each change quotes the code as it reads today, the proposed text, and the reason. Each change ends with who it affects. Staff costs are not estimated here; the City should price them before the Council votes.

### Sec. 12-8(a) and (b): purpose, definitions and who is responsible

Name the terms the rule depends on, and put one name on every ticket: the owner of record.

**Purpose**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "Sidewalks, curb ramps, crosswalks and bus stops are part of the city's transportation network. This section keeps them passable in winter for everyone, including people who use wheelchairs, walkers, canes or strollers."
- *Why:* Gives inspectors and the Council a test for gray areas: can a person using a wheelchair get through?

**Sidewalk**

- *Today:* Not defined.
- *Proposed:* "'Sidewalk' means the paved way between the property line and the curb, including driveway crossings."
- *Why:* WalkBoston's statewide review names corners and ramps as the worst trouble spots in winter. So ramps get their own rule in (c)(2) and are named beside sidewalks throughout; the definition adds driveway crossings, which are part of the path.

**Storm**

- *Today:* "following the conclusion of a weather event" (not defined).
- *Proposed:* "'Storm' means any snow, sleet or freezing rain, however light. It ends when it stops falling in the city or, if a snow emergency is declared, when the snow emergency ends."
- *Why:* "Storm" is the plain word, and "however light" keeps every snowfall covered, as today. Freezing rain or sleet fell in 20 of 44 snow periods since December 2015. Snow emergencies are the ones the City already declares for parking and plowing; the ordinance adds no new kind. In an emergency, the announced end counts as the storm's end, so no one argues about when the snow stopped. Ice that forms later from melting is not a storm, since no one can see when it formed: the duty to keep the path clear covers it, and ice left untreated is a $300 ticket.

**Other terms**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "'Priority routes' means the bus routes, squares and school walking routes named in the winter plan under Section 12-8B(b). 'City property' means land or buildings the City owns, including schools, parks, libraries, fire stations and parking lots, and the bridges and Community Path segments the City maintains. 'Clear' means free of snow and ice down to bare pavement, whatever the source: snowfall, roofs, plows, wind or a neighbor. Sand or de-icer on snow does not make it clear. 'Bus stop' means the sidewalk and curb from a bus stop sign back 40 feet, and any shelter or bench there."
- *Why:* Each term sets a duty, a fee or a fine: where inspectors patrol and crews start, how many clearing fees a corner lot pays (one per 25 feet of each side) and where snow may not go at a stop. The winter plan names the routes, so they can change without amending the ordinance.

**Who is responsible**

- *Today:* "A person, including an owner, occupant, tenant, or agent, in charge of land or a building"
- *Proposed:* "'Owner' means the owner of record in the City's assessing records; for a condominium, its association." and "The owner is responsible, whatever a lease says, even if away or using a contractor."
- *Why:* Finding out who controls a property (owner, tenant, manager, business) takes an investigation the City can't afford on a $100 ticket, and a ticket to the wrong person starts an appeal. The owner is in the City's records in seconds, the lien already attaches to the property, and the City never tickets or bills a tenant, answering the March 2025 letter's request that fines not be passed on to tenants. A tenant may be unable to shovel, and has no say over a contractor who misses. State law aims the sidewalk penalty at "the owner of abutting property or his agent" (M.G.L. c. 40 §21(3)). Washington, DC lets an owner delegate in writing but keeps the owner liable; Boston tickets a tenant only when a written lease expressly assigns the duty (CBC 16-12.16). A city can't rewrite leases (Attachment C), so this binds the City's enforcement, and Attachment B asks the state to protect tenants in the lease itself. It mirrors the State Sanitary Code, which keeps shared exits with the landlord and lets a lease shift only a tenant's own exit, in writing ([105 CMR 410.260](https://www.law.cornell.edu/regulations/massachusetts/105-CMR-410-260)). The state's Office on Disability reads it the same way: in a multi-family building, "Sidewalks are not the renter's responsibility" (2025).

**Which sidewalks**

- *Today:* "an abutting sidewalk"
- *Proposed:* "a continuous path along each sidewalk the property abuts, both sides of a corner lot included"
- *Why:* "An abutting sidewalk" is singular; naming both sides of a corner lot removes an easy argument on appeal.

**Being away**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(b): "even if away".
- *Why:* Brookline and Cambridge already tell residents this (see Sources). Writing it in the code removes an argument on appeal.

**Who is affected**

- **Homeowners:** no change; they get their own tickets, as today. Condominium associations get the ticket for their building, as they already handle its shared parts.
- **Tenants:** never ticketed or billed by the City for sidewalk snow.
- **Landlords and other owners:** shovel or hire someone for every rental, and pay every ticket. The cost may show up in rents.
- **City staff:** no more investigating who is in charge, and fewer appeals over the wrong person.

### Sec. 12-8(c): the path and driveways

Widen the path to 4 feet, make bare pavement the rule and sand the stopgap, keep it clear, ice that forms later included, and clear the whole driveway crossing.

**Clear path**

- *Today:* "a path of travel with a minimum width of thirty-six inches"
- *Proposed:* "The owner must clear, and keep clear: (1) a continuous path along each sidewalk the property abuts, both sides of a corner lot included, at least 48 inches wide (or the whole sidewalk if narrower), connecting to the neighbors' paths and to curb ramps (the 'clear path')." "Clear" means down to bare pavement (Sec. 12-8(a)).
- *Why:* Width is explained under "Width" below. "Continuous" and "connecting" stop the gap-at-the-property-line problem.

**Width**

- *Today:* "a path of travel with a minimum width of thirty-six inches"
- *Proposed:* "at least 48 inches wide (or the whole sidewalk if narrower)"
- *Why:* PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for the entire width, as in Minneapolis; we propose one number instead, which is easier to follow and to measure. 36 inches is the bare minimum for one wheelchair, with no room to pass or turn. Federal guidelines set 48 inches for new and altered public sidewalks (PROWAG R302.2, 2023); 36 inches is the ADA minimum for a walking surface. Boston requires 42 inches; Worcester requires 4 ft. Somerville's own code already keeps 4 ft clear of newsracks (Sec. 12-69(1)). MassDOT records a sidewalk width of 4 feet or more for 98% of Somerville's sidewalk miles that have a recorded width (2025 Road Inventory, in whole feet). The clear path around poles, trees and hydrants can be narrower, which is why the rule allows the whole sidewalk where it is narrower.

**Finish**

- *Today:* "clear the sidewalk to its surface, or treat it with sand or other suitable material where clearing to the surface is impractical"
- *Proposed:* "'Clear' means free of snow and ice down to bare pavement, whatever the source: snowfall, roofs, plows, wind or a neighbor. Sand or de-icer on snow does not make it clear." (Sec. 12-8(a)) and "Ice that cannot be removed must be leveled and covered with sand or de-icer." (Sec. 12-8(c))
- *Why:* Today a sprinkle of sand over packed snow can pass as compliance. A thin layer left behind freezes into the worst ice. State guidance asks for snow and ice cleared "down to the pavement and treated with an ice-melt" (Massachusetts Office on Disability, 2025). "Whatever the source" ends the argument that a plow or a neighbor put it there, as Madison's code does ("regardless of the source of accumulation") and Cambridge's (snow from buildings "as well as … from the clouds").

**Keeping it clear**

- *Today:* None: the duty ends at the deadline.
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(c), the owner must "clear, and keep clear". That includes ice that forms later from melting and refreezing or from water off a building.
- *Why:* Reports keep coming for a median of 6 days after the snow. Melt-and-refreeze ice causes slips long after the storm; PTAC members found ice on sidewalks up to 7 days after the snow in winter 2024–25.

**Driveway crossings**

- *Today:* No separate rule.
- *Proposed:* "(7) where a driveway crosses the sidewalk, the whole driveway from the property line to the street, apron included", cleared to the same standard as the sidewalk (Sec. 12-8(c)).
- *Why:* Where a driveway crosses the sidewalk it slopes toward the street, so meltwater runs across it and refreezes. A 48-inch path leaves ice beside it on the slope, where people step to let others pass, and snow left on the apron slides back onto the walk.

**Who is affected**

- **All owners:** a 4-ft path instead of 3 ft; sand or salt on hand; a return trip after refreezes and plows; snow piled on their own property or hauled away at their cost, plow banks over 3½ feet in the planted strip included, which matters most on small lots. Parking-lot contractors can't push snow across walks; some will haul it.
- **Tenants and everyone on foot:** a wider, safer path.
- **City staff:** fewer hydrants and drains to dig out.

### Sec. 12-8(c): corners, crosswalks, bus stops, hydrants, parking and icicles

A cleared sidewalk is useless if the corner is a snowbank. Extend the ramp duty to every corner property, add an opening through the plow bank, and say who clears each bus stop.

**Curb ramps**

- *Today:* (a)(1): only "a building with exclusively commercial units or a building with ground floor commercial units abutting an accessible curb ramp shall also clear the curb ramp to provide ... clear and direct access to the crosswalk"
- *Proposed:* "(2) each abutting curb ramp, with its landing and raised warning surface, and a 48-inch path from it through any plow bank to the street (the 'crosswalk opening')." Owners who qualify get help under (f) instead of a ticket.
- *Why:* Ramps sit at corners, and many corners are homes, where the ordinance requires no one to clear them. The City's snow page already tells residents and businesses they are "responsible for" clearing "accessible ramps that abut their property", a duty the ordinance places only on businesses. Brookline's by-law requires every owner, homes included, to keep corner and midblock curb ramps clear (Art. 7.7, amended 2022); Madison's requires corner owners to clear the sidewalk bordering the crosswalk, "including the curb ramp" (General Ordinances 10.28(1)); Boston's code gives curb ramps the same 3-hour deadline as sidewalks (CBC 16-12.16(C)), though its ramp fine covers only non-residential property; Cambridge tells corner owners they must, and New York City's 311 rules require the same; Worcester asks. Ord. 2025-25 (Dec 2025) covered businesses only; this finishes the job. The raised warning domes tell people who are blind where the street begins, and federal guidelines call for a 48-inch-square clear area at the bottom of every ramp (PROWAG R305, R304.2.4). State guidance agrees: "If the abutting sidewalk includes a curb cut, the property owner is responsible for clearing the curb cut as well" (Massachusetts Office on Disability, 2025, citing c. 40 §21(3)).

**Crosswalk opening**

- *Today:* Implied only for businesses ("clear and direct access to the crosswalk").
- *Proposed:* Part of (c)(2): "a 48-inch path from it through any plow bank to the street", at every ramp, marked crosswalk or not.
- *Why:* A clear ramp that ends at a 3-foot plow bank still traps a wheelchair. Boston's code (CBC 16-12.16(C)) requires a 42-inch path at curb ramps "from the sidewalk to the street"; Brookline asks for crosswalk openings, and Cambridge holds owners responsible for crosswalk access and tickets them (74 tickets in 2025–26).

**Bus stops**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "(3) a strip at least 48 inches wide along the curb of any bus stop in front of the property, connected to the clear path." A bus stop runs from the sign back 40 feet (Sec. 12-8(a)).
- *Why:* Riders step off into snowbanks. In Somerville the MBTA clears stops only on routes 77, 104 and 109, within 24 hours; the City clears its 281 stops third in its clearing order, after streets and City lots, with no time set. Ann Arbor makes abutters clear bus stop pads, and New York City's 311 rules require owners to clear the walk at bus stops. This asks less: a 48-inch strip at the curb, while the boarding area, shelter and bench stay with the City or the MBTA (Sec. 12-8A, 12-8B(b)(5)). A strip, not one opening, because buses can't always stop in the same spot: the MBTA's Bus Stop Planning & Design Guide (2018) calls for a clear zone at each door and prefers "a contiguous 4' deep clear zone" from the front door to the back of the stop, for 40-foot and 60-foot buses.

**Hydrants**

- *Today:* None. The City's snow page says crews shovel out hydrants and welcomes help from residents who are able "(3 feet in all directions)".
- *Proposed:* "No one … may put snow or ice … within 3 feet of a hydrant" and "(4) 3 feet around any hydrant on its sidewalk."
- *Why:* Longmeadow's bylaw (2017 text) requires the adjacent owner to clear a hydrant within 24 hours; New York City's 311 rules require owners to clear around hydrants. Owners already clear the sidewalk beside the hydrant; the circle adds a few minutes of shoveling and spares City crews the digging.

**Accessible parking**

- *Today:* None for snow.
- *Proposed:* "(5) on a property with a parking lot, whenever the lot is cleared, its accessible parking spaces, their access aisles and the route to the entrance" and "No one … may put snow or ice … on … accessible parking space or aisle"
- *Why:* Federal rules already require businesses to keep accessible features usable (28 CFR 36.211). The Justice Department's guidance says to clear accessible spaces "whenever plowing or clearing the rest of the parking area" and not to pile snow in them. Putting it in the code lets inspectors enforce it.

**Falling ice and snow**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(c)(6): "icicles, and snow or ice hanging over the edge of a roof, awning or sign above a sidewalk", due on the sidewalk deadlines, and "Until icicles and overhanging snow or ice are removed, the owner must keep the sidewalk below marked with a sign, cones or tape."
- *Why:* Icicles and roof slides hit people on the sidewalk below. State law lets cities require this (M.G.L. c. 40 §21(2)); Chelsea's ordinance (2012 text) gives owners 24 hours after the snow falls or the ice forms (Sec. 6-124(b)), a time no inspector can see. Here the clock is the sidewalk's (Sec. 12-8(d)). What must go is what anyone can see from the sidewalk: icicles and overhanging snow or ice.

**Who is affected**

- **Homeowners:** corner homes clear their ramps and a crosswalk opening; the few at bus stops clear a 48-inch strip along the curb, 40 feet back from the sign; owners with a hydrant keep 3 feet clear on every side of it. Eligible seniors and owners with disabilities get help instead.
- **Landlords and other owners:** the same on their corners and stops (owners of commercial buildings already must clear ramps); owners of parking lots also clear accessible spaces, aisles and the route to the door when the lot is plowed. Every owner removes icicles and overhanging snow or ice from a roof, awning or sign above the sidewalk.
- **City staff:** fewer ramps, bus stops and hydrants for City crews to clear.

### Sec. 12-8(d): when

Two cases. In ordinary snowfall, owners **keep the path, ramps and other cleared areas under 1 inch** while it snows, from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., and clear it to bare pavement within 3 hours after it stops, but no later than 10 p.m. (by 10 a.m. if it stops between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m.). In a declared **snow emergency**, nothing is due until the City announces the end; then owners have 3 hours, up to 10 p.m., or until 10 a.m. if it ends between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. No one has to shovel between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m.

**Snowfall**

- *Today:* "may not ... allow it to remain ... for more than six hours between sunrise and sunset following the conclusion of a weather event"
- *Proposed:* "Subsection (c) is due within 3 hours after the storm ends, but no later than 10 p.m.; if it ends between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., it is due by 10 a.m." and "While snow is falling from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., nothing subsection (c) requires clearing may hold more than 1 inch of snow or slush."
- *Why:* Today's clock starts at "the conclusion of a weather event", a time no one can see, and two days of light snow set no deadline at all. While it snows, the limit needs only a ruler: an inspector measures the path, whatever the time. A heavier snowfall means more trips outside; a dusting means none. Once the snow stops, 3 hours clear what is left before it freezes. State guidance asks for snow and ice cleared "down to the pavement" and says accessible features must be "kept fully operational at all times" (Massachusetts Office on Disability, 2025). PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for clearing during long, light snowfalls: people keep walking, and their steps pack soft snow into ice.

**Snow emergencies**

- *Today:* The same clock as any storm; a declared snow emergency changes only parking rules (Sec. 1-11(b)).
- *Proposed:* The same deadline as any storm (Sec. 12-8(d)(2)): a storm in a snow emergency ends when the City announces the end of the emergency (Sec. 12-8(a)(3)), so owners have 3 hours from then, up to 10 p.m., or until 10 a.m. if it ends between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m. Under (1), nothing is due while it lasts.
- *Why:* In a declared emergency, no one should be out shoveling in the storm. The City already declares these storms and announces their end, so that announced end starts the clock: no one has to compute sunrise or argue about when the snow stopped. The City's own notices already gave a clearing time: 12:30 p.m. in February 2022 and "1 p.m." in February 2026. The rule makes it every emergency.

**Hours and days**

- *Today:* Applies to any "weather event".
- *Proposed:* Keep, and add: "No one is required to clear between 10 p.m. and 7 a.m., or while a snow emergency is in effect."
- *Why:* Residents tell PTAC that small storms leave the worst ice because people skip them; the code should say plainly that every storm counts. No one has to shovel at night; New York City doesn't count 9 p.m. to 7 a.m. either (Admin. Code 16-123(a)).

**Plow re-burial**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* No separate rule: "clear" covers snow "whatever the source" (Sec. 12-8(a)), plows included, cleared on the same schedule as any other.
- *Why:* Residents ask whether the clock restarts. In ordinary snowfall, plow snow counts toward the 1 inch and is cleared like any other snow. In an emergency, plows work while the duty is paused, and the deadline runs from when the City announces the end. Drifts don't restart it: no one can tell when a drift formed, so it would be an excuse no inspector could check. Snow an owner puts back is already banned by (e). Brookline gives owners of buildings with more than 4 units 6 more hours to re-clear corners the Town's plows fill in (Art. 7.7.2).

**Extensions**

- *Today:* "or as otherwise extended by the city in its discretion"
- *Proposed:* Strike it. Only a declared snow emergency pauses the duty, and the City announces its end (Sec. 12-8(d)(2), 12-8B(c)).
- *Why:* The code doesn't say how or where an extension is announced, so residents can't know whether one applies to them. Brookline's by-law requires any delay to be posted on the Town's home page and sent through social media, cable TV, email lists and the emergency phone system (Art. 7.7.6); a snow emergency is already the City's public signal for a big storm, and Sec. 12-8B(c) announces its end on those same channels.

**Today's rule vs. proposed, for a storm on Jan 15 that is not a snow emergency** (today's column from [PTAC's deadline calculator](https://somerwalks.org/snow/#deadline), which follows Sec. 12-8):

While it snows, the path, ramps and other cleared areas may never hold more than 1 inch from 8 a.m. to 10 p.m. In a snow emergency, the 3 hours start when the City announces the end, with the same 10 p.m. limit, or 10 a.m. if it ends overnight.

**Who is affected**

- **All owners:** never more than 1 inch on the path, ramps and other cleared areas while it snows, 8 a.m. to 10 p.m., and bare pavement within 3 hours after it stops, by 10 p.m. at the latest (by 10 a.m. if it stops overnight); in a snow emergency, the 3 hours start when the City announces the end. No one shovels at night.
- **City staff:** no daylight hours to compute; announce the start and end of each snow emergency; inspections while it snows, not only after.

### Sec. 12-8(e): where snow and objects may not go

Keep snow off the places people walk, wait and cross, and out of drains.

**Where snow goes**

- *Today:* "may not place snow or ice ... on an abutting sidewalk"
- *Proposed:* "(1) No one, contractors included, may put snow or ice on a sidewalk or anywhere else subsection (c) requires clearing, on a crosswalk, bike lane, storm drain, push-button pole, or accessible parking space or aisle, within 3 feet of a hydrant, or on a street except as paragraph (2) allows." and "(3) Snow in the planted strip between the sidewalk and the curb, plow snow included, may be no higher than 3½ feet above the sidewalk." Streets, (e)(2), are under Sec. 12-9 below; bins, (e)(4), under Sec. 12-18. The ticket goes to the owner of the property the snow or ice came from (Sec. 1-11(d)(1)).
- *Why:* Today the ban covers only one's own abutting sidewalk, not contractors who push lot snow across a walk. Boston bans placing snow from private property on any sidewalk, curb ramp or street (CBC 16-12.16(E)); Brookline bans it on any street or sidewalk, with a $250 fine (Art. 7.7.4–7.7.5). Piles at corners hide children and wheelchair users from drivers. A height an inspector can measure replaces "block the view", a judgment call. 3½ feet within 20 feet of a corner is the limit Somerville's former zoning code set on fences and plantings so drivers can see across corners (SZO §8.6.5.c, quoted in the City's 2017 staff report on 1 Howe St); Olean, NY caps snow piles on corner lots at 2½ feet (Sec. 21-5). Snow past the cap goes onto the owner's land or is hauled away, so the piles at the curb stay small. The cap covers plow banks too, as the crosswalk opening in (c)(2) already does, so the inspector measures without asking where the snow came from. Snow piled against a signal pole puts the crossing button out of reach; federal guidelines place it within 5 feet of the ramp (PROWAG R307). State guidance says cleared snow should not go "into the road, bike lane, crosswalk, curb cuts, accessible parking spaces or adjacent access aisles" (Massachusetts Office on Disability, 2025); (e)(2) still lets broken-up snow go in the street, within limits.

**Storm drains**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "No one … may put snow or ice … on a … storm drain"
- *Why:* Meltwater that can't drain refreezes across the ramp and crosswalk.

### Sec. 12-8(f): help for those who can't shovel

Pair a wider duty with real help for people who can't shovel.

**Help for those who can't**

- *Today:* None. The City runs a paid teen-shoveler program ($25, or $60 for a corner lot, in 2024/25, per The Somerville Times).
- *Proposed:* "The City must run a snow assistance program. An owner who lives in a 1- to 3-unit building, is 65 or older or has a disability, and earns less than a limit the City sets may register at any time. Registering by December 1 covers the whole winter, October 1 to May 1. The City, its volunteers or organizations it funds clear what subsection (c) requires for registered properties, at no charge to the owner, by the deadlines of subsection (d). A registered property is not ticketed under this section."
- *Why:* A wider duty (corner ramps, a wider path, continuing ice) must not fall on residents who physically can't do it. Cambridge's City staff clear the sidewalk for some low-income older or disabled homeowners (41 homes in 2025–26). Washington, DC does not fine residential owners who are 65 or older or have a disability. The state's Office on Disability recommends that cities run a snow removal assistance program, and names Somerville's Teen Snow Shoveling Program as an example (2025).

**Volunteers**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In the winter plan, Sec. 12-8B(b)(7): "a public sign-up for neighbors who adopt a curb ramp, a bus stop or a registered neighbor's sidewalk".
- *Why:* A public list lets the City know which ramps and stops are covered, send help where none is, and thank the volunteers. Paying neighborhood groups is an option, not a mandate: Minneapolis has paid neighborhood associations to clear sidewalks in its winter pilots, and all its 2024 pilots together cost about $230,000 (Axios, June 2025). New York City's code has its Sanitation Department help community groups run volunteer registries for residents who can't shovel (Admin. Code 16-135).

**Who is affected**

- **All owners:** arrange help before traveling. Eligible owners in the assistance program are cleared and not fined.
- **City staff:** run a new assistance program (registration, crews, volunteers or funded neighborhood groups, a budget).

### Sec. 12-8(g): construction sites

Construction sites are covered in principle today, since the duty falls on whoever is "in charge of land or a building". In practice no one is around to do it, and the permits that let a site use the sidewalk say nothing about winter. Tie the duty to the permit.

**Winter permit condition**

- *Today:* The ordinance sets no winter conditions for dumpster (Sec. 12-77), obstruction (Sec. 12-12) or sidewalk-opening (Sec. 12-20) permits; any conditions are left to each permit.
- *Proposed:* "Every permit to place a dumpster, obstruct or open a sidewalk, or build or demolish, includes this condition: clear and keep clear what subsection (c) requires, by the deadlines of subsection (d)."
- *Why:* Sec. 12-77 already treats breaking a permit condition as a violation; this puts snow in the conditions.

**Sidewalk closures**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "A permit that closes a sidewalk must provide an accessible detour at least 48 inches wide, signed before the closure, edged with barriers a cane can detect, and kept clear like a sidewalk under subsections (c) and (d)."
- *Why:* A closed sidewalk with an unplowed detour pushes walkers and wheelchair users into the street. Federal guidelines ask for the same signs and cane-detectable edges (PROWAG R303); PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for clearly marked, cleared detours.

**Paying for City clearing**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* No separate rule. An unpaid clearing fee becomes a lien under Sec. 12-8(h), as for any owner.
- *Why:* Only some permits carry a deposit: Sec. 12-20 holds a $500 deposit for street and sidewalk openings, but the ordinance sets none for construction dumpsters (Sec. 12-77) or building permits. The lien already secures payment.

**Holding the permit**

- *Today:* Sec. 1-11(c): unpaid fines may block local licenses and permits.
- *Proposed:* "After 3 tickets for the property while a permit is open, the City may suspend a dumpster, obstruction or sidewalk-opening permit until the sidewalk is clear and the tickets are paid."
- *Why:* A suspended dumpster or sidewalk permit costs a project far more than any ticket the state allows, and the City issues those permits itself. Holding building inspections instead would run into the State Building Code (Attachment C). Minneapolis makes failure to clear grounds to revoke the City license of a business whose licensed operation needs a driveway or curb cut (Code 445.40).

**Who is affected**

- **Developers:** winter permit conditions, signed detours and $300 tickets.
- **City staff:** add the condition to permits and track tickets per permit.

### Sec. 12-8(h) and (i): when the City clears and bills

A ticket doesn't clear ice. Turn "may clear" into a set time for re-inspection and City clearing, so every ticketed sidewalk ends up open.

**City clearing**

- *Today:* "Upon neglect or violation of the duty ... such duty may be performed by the superintendent of highways at the expense of the person liable"
- *Proposed:* "A sidewalk still not clear gets a new ticket, and the Commissioner of Public Works must clear it within the next 24 hours."
- *Why:* "May" with no time sets no expectation: residents can't tell whether or when the City will step in. The City's own snow page gives two different start times. "Superintendent of highways" is an old title. The City clears after the second ticket, so every owner gets a day to fix a first miss, and the re-inspection the ticket already requires tells DPW which walks to clear. Corners on priority routes can't wait two days, so the City opens them within 12 hours of the first ticket, an idea Cambridge's Council asked the City to explore in 2018. New York City's code lets the City clear a neglected sidewalk and add the cost to the owner's next tax bill (Admin. Code 16-123(d)–(e)). Minneapolis may clear any sidewalk still covered 24 hours after the snow stops and assess unpaid bills on the property (Code 445.35), and Madison charges its clearing to the property as a special charge (General Ordinances 10.28(2)).

**Re-inspection**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The inspector re-inspects within 24 hours after each ticket."
- *Why:* Ticketing (ISD) and clearing (DPW) sit in different departments, and the ordinance sets no handoff between them. Sec. 1-11(a) already counts each day as a new offense; this makes it happen.

**Cost**

- *Today:* "at the expense of the person liable ... and the city may place a lien against the property for expenses incurred"
- *Proposed:* "The owner also pays a flat fee, set at the City's average cost, for each 25 feet of sidewalk and each curb ramp the City clears. An unpaid fee becomes a lien, split among the units of a condominium. Tickets, City clearing and removal do not bar each other."
- *Why:* A flat fee is known in advance and needs no invoice per job; set it to cover the City's average cost. Charging by each 25 feet, the way New York City counts a "lot" for snow violations (Admin. Code 16-123(g)), keeps a long corner sidewalk from costing the same as a narrow one. Saying that the ticket and the clearing don't bar each other, as Madison's code does (General Ordinances 10.28(2)), stops an owner from arguing one replaces the other. Unpaid fees become a lien, as today's Sec. 12-8(c) already allows for the City's clearing costs.

**Collections**

- *Today:* (d) "The city treasurer with the approval of the mayor may in civil actions prosecute and adjust claims"
- *Proposed:* Keep unchanged.
- *Why:* Works as is.

**Who is affected**

- **Owners:** pay the City's flat clearing fee when its crews have to clear.
- **City staff:** return visits within 24 hours of a ticket; DPW crews clear more sidewalks; the fee recovers the cost.

### Sec. 12-8(j): liability and legal basis

Keep enforcement with the City, and cite the state law that allows today's fines.

**Liability**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "The duties in this section and in Sections 12-8A and 12-8B are enforced by the City. They create no private right of action and do not enlarge the liability of the City or of any owner beyond what state law provides."
- *Why:* Firm City deadlines could narrow a defense the City has today (Attachment C). Injury claims stay under state law, c. 84 §17 and the Tort Claims Act, and no one gains a new right to sue the City or a neighbor.

**State law reference**

- *Today:* Sec. 12-8 cites both c. 40 §21 and c. 85 §5.
- *Proposed:* "M.G.L. c. 40, §21(2)–(4) and §21D", replacing the reference to c. 85 §5.
- *Why:* c. 85 §5 caps a city's fine at $50, below today's fines. c. 40 §21 lets cities require sidewalk clearing, and §21D allows tickets of up to $300. See Attachment C.

### New Sec. 12-8A: City bus stops, ramps and accessible parking

Residents have a clearing deadline; the City has none. Hold City property to the owners' rule, and set a clock for the bus stops, ramps and parking the City clears.

**City property**

- *Today:* None. Sec. 12-8 names "a person ... in charge of land or a building"; whether it binds the City is unclear.
- *Proposed:* No separate rule: Sec. 12-8 applies to the City as owner of its property, tickets included.
- *Why:* The rule residents follow should be the rule the City follows; today the ordinance is silent on sidewalks along City property. Washington, DC's code makes the city clear its own sidewalks and crosswalks within 8 daylight hours. Boston tickets its own properties too ("We fine ourselves for City-owned properties too," Boston Public Works, "Rules for clearing snow"), and once its parks crews logged their clearing, violations at City-owned properties fell from 150–200 per storm to 8. Even after a city assigns clearing to owners, it is "ultimately legally responsible for ensuring that all public areas meet accessibility regulations" (Massachusetts Office on Disability, 2025).

**Bus stops**

- *Today:* None. The snow page says the City clears 281 stops, "divided into 4 Routes", third in its clearing order, after streets and City lots.
- *Proposed:* "The City must clear the following within 24 hours after the storm ends, 12 hours on priority routes, and again within 24 hours after its plows bury them: (1) at the bus stops the winter plan under Section 12-8B(b) assigns to it, a boarding area 8 feet deep and 5 feet wide (or the whole sidewalk if narrower) with the gutter in front of it, any shelter or bench, and 3 feet around a shelter's open sides out to the curb; (2) curb ramps and crosswalk openings that no private property abuts …; (3) accessible parking spaces on its streets and in its lots … On priority routes, the City clears a curb ramp or crosswalk opening within 12 hours after a ticket for it, without waiting for the re-inspection under Section 12-8(h). While a storm lasts, stops on routes that are running must be kept passable by 8 a.m. and by 4 p.m."
- *Why:* Riders need a time they can count on. The MBTA already commits to 24 hours for the stops it clears. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked that stops be clear whenever buses are running. The 8-by-5-foot boarding area is the federal standard for a bus stop (2010 ADA Standards 810.2.2; PROWAG R309). Ottawa's volunteer winter audits asked for cleared benches too, for riders who can't stand while they wait (Council on Aging of Ottawa, 2022). The gutter, where riders step, and the space around shelters follow New York City's code (Admin. Code 16-124.1(a)). One clock covers every place the City clears: bus stops now also get cleared again after plows bury them, and ramps and accessible parking get the 12-hour time on priority routes.

**Which stops are the City's**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* See new Sec. 12-8A and 12-8B(b): the City clears the boarding area at the stops the winter plan assigns to it within 24 hours (12 on priority routes), and again after its plows bury them, and the plan's map shows which stops are the City's and which are the MBTA's.
- *Why:* The City counts 281 stops it clears; the MBTA lists 207 stops in Somerville. Which stops are whose job is not published, so a rider can't tell who to call, or when to expect it.

**Ramps with no owner to clear them**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* Part of the City's single clock in Sec. 12-8A: "(2) curb ramps and crosswalk openings that no private property abuts, pedestrian refuge islands, and openings through the snow ridges plows leave between bike lanes and crosswalks", cleared within 24 hours, 12 on priority routes, and again after plows bury them. Owners still clear the ramps they abut (Sec. 12-8(c)(2)).
- *Why:* The City's snow page lists curb cuts and intersection landings among the City's work, with no deadline and no list of which ones. Cambridge's Council made crossing islands a priority in 2025, and its Public Works department committed that year to opening crossing islands and the snow ridges beside separated bike lanes at crosswalks.

**Signal push buttons and accessible parking**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8A(2): "snow at pedestrian push buttons, which stay reachable from the cleared curb ramp"; and "(3) accessible parking spaces on its streets and in its lots, including snow emergency lots" join the City's single clock in Sec. 12-8A.
- *Why:* These are the City's own accessible features, and federal rules require it to keep them working, allowing only brief interruptions (28 CFR 35.133). Street plows bury curbside spaces again, so the duty repeats after they do, as for curb ramps (Sec. 12-8A). The spaces go on the clearing log and its public map (Sec. 12-8B(b)(4)), so anyone can see whether the City met its 24 hours.

**Who is affected**

- **Everyone:** a set schedule for City bus stops, ramps and accessible parking, and City sidewalks held to the owners' rule.
- **City staff:** binding deadlines and more crew hours, with liability questions for the Law Department. Crews also keep signal push buttons reachable and accessible parking spaces clear.

### New Sec. 12-8B: coordination, winter plan and reporting

Put one official in charge of coordinating the whole effort, publish a winter plan, tell people when each snow emergency ends and when clearing is due, and publish what enforcement and City crews actually did, so the Council can judge whether the rules work.

**One lead**

- *Today:* Enforcement is split among DPW, Inspectional Services and Traffic and Parking (Sec. 1-11(b)), with no lead named.
- *Proposed:* "The Mayor must name one official (the lead official) to coordinate the City's snow and ice program across departments and with owners, volunteers and outside agencies: the winter plan, public information, inspections and enforcement, data and the season report."
- *Why:* PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for one team to lead. What owners, volunteers, agencies and City crews do adds up to one result, and someone has to answer for all of it. Public Works runs the crews and is busiest during a storm, while the plan, notices, inspections, data and the report span other departments (enforcement alone is split three ways). Residents, the Council and PTAC would have one person to call.

**A plan with times**

- *Today:* None. The snow page lists an eight-step clearing order with no times.
- *Proposed:* "By October 1 each year, the lead official must publish a draft winter plan for comment, and by November 1 the final plan. The City carries out the plan, which sets out:" (1) the priority routes, with how they were chosen and what changed; (2) the clearing order, a target time for streets, and a map of who clears each bus stop; and (3) to (7) the de-icer, clearing log, agreements, season guide and neighbor sign-up described below.
- *Why:* Naming the priority routes tells inspectors and crews where to start. Bus stops, ramps and sidewalks along City property already get deadlines in Sec. 12-8 and 12-8A; streets get a target time. Today residents can't tell whether a stop is late or not on any list. New York City's code requires a snow plan for each borough every November 15, with a map of priority streets, how curb cuts, pedestrian medians, crosswalks and bus stops will be cleared, and the official responsible, after a draft goes out for comment by October 1 (Admin. Code 16-124.1).

**Free de-icer and pre-treatment**

- *Today:* None in the code. 311 takes "Sand barrel" requests (500 since 2015).
- *Proposed:* In the winter plan, Sec. 12-8B(b)(3): "free sand or de-icer for residents at published locations, announced each November, and pre-treatment of bus stops, curb ramps and sidewalks along City property on priority routes before forecast ice."
- *Why:* PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for free ice melt and more pre-treatment. Ice causes the worst falls, and treating early is cheaper than chipping later.

**Proof of clearing**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In the winter plan, Sec. 12-8B(b)(4): "a clearing log of when each bus stop, curb ramp, accessible parking space and sidewalk along City property is cleared (by GPS where available), shown on a public map during and after each storm".
- *Why:* Boston's parks crews did this with a simple form; inspection time per storm fell from 30 hours to 6 (Boston Analytics Team). GPS makes the log automatic, so crews don't stop to fill in forms. Toronto's public plow map drew 230,000 views in one winter; its lesson is to show a street as cleared only when it is (City of Toronto staff report, Nov 2025).

**State and MBTA property**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In the winter plan, Sec. 12-8B(b)(5): "the written agreements the City seeks with the MBTA, the Massachusetts Department of Transportation and the Department of Conservation and Recreation on who clears their bus stops, station entrances and sidewalks in the city, and by when, including how stops stay passable while buses run during a storm", published with the plan.
- *Why:* The City lists State property last in its clearing order, with no contact. PTAC could not find who clears the sidewalks along McGrath Highway or the Green Line station entrances.

**Emergency notices**

- *Today:* None required by the code. The City posts snow emergency notices and occasional sidewalk reminders.
- *Proposed:* "The City must announce the start and end of each snow emergency, with the owners' deadline after the emergency (Section 12-8(d)(2)) as a clock time, by City Alert and its other public channels, in the languages of its snow rules."
- *Why:* In ordinary snowfall, the rule is the same in every storm, so no notice is needed. A snow emergency is the one time the duty changes, so its end and the clearing deadline must reach everyone, on the channels Brookline's by-law uses for delays (Art. 7.7.6). The City already announces emergencies, and its February 2026 emergency notices stated a clearing time.

**Season guide**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* In the winter plan, Sec. 12-8B(b)(6): "a season guide sent each November to every household and business, with the rules, photographs or illustrations of good and bad clearing, how to report a missed sidewalk, and the help available".
- *Why:* Residents tell PTAC the first December storm is when people relearn the rules; a November mailing reaches them first, including everyone who moved in since last winter. PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for this content, noting that the City's snow materials focus on cars.

**How a 311 report ends**

- *Today:* Every sidewalk report reads "Closed".
- *Proposed:* "A 311 request about an uncleared sidewalk must be closed with its outcome: clear on inspection, ticket, cleared by the City, registered property cleared by the City, sidewalk along City property, or not found."
- *Why:* Today "Closed" can mean anything; 754 cases were closed on one day, Feb 6, 2026, 663 of them over a month old.

**Open data**

- *Today:* 311 requests only.
- *Proposed:* "By the same date, the City must publish the season's records on its open data portal: inspections and tickets under Sections 12-2, 12-8 and 12-18(e), with date, time, street and ward but no house number; City clearing and liens under Section 12-8(h); the clearing log under Section 12-8B(b)(4); and each declared snow emergency with its start and end times."
- *Why:* None of this is public today. Patterns (blocks that fail every storm, wards no one inspects) stay invisible. Publishing once, with the annual report, means one export a year.

**Annual report**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* "By May 1 each year, the lead official must report to the City Council on the past winter season: complaints, inspections, tickets, City clearings and liens, sidewalks along City property found not clear, and how long the City took to clear bus stops, curb ramps and sidewalks along City property. It also gives the season's costs of snow and ice clearing, including City crews, contractors, de-icer and the snow assistance program, and the fines and fees collected; and it recommends any changes to these ordinances or the City's snow regulations for the next winter."
- *Why:* Lets the Council see whether these changes work, and adjust them before the next winter, with the lead official's recommendations in hand. The costs show what clearing takes and what fines bring in, which the Council needs to fund it. Brookline's by-law has required a yearly report on education, enforcement and help since at least 2012 (Art. 7.7.7). It can join the Complete Streets progress report the Council already receives under Sec. 12-116.6, which also tracks the City's ADA Transition Plan goals.

**Blocked accessible routes**

- *Today:* None. Every 311 sidewalk report goes into the same queue.
- *Proposed:* "311 must offer a request type for a blocked curb ramp, crosswalk opening, bus stop or path that keeps a person with a disability from leaving home or reaching transit. These requests go to the City's ADA Coordinator, and the person who asked gets a response, with a time for clearing, within 12 hours."
- *Why:* A blocked ramp can keep a wheelchair user at home for days. The City must already name an ADA Coordinator and keep a way to resolve complaints (28 CFR 35.107); this gives winter complaints a fast lane. The state's Office on Disability names the municipal ADA Coordinator and the local Commission on Disability as the places to take snow problems that keep recurring (2025).

**Accessible formats**

- *Today:* None.
- *Proposed:* No rule in the ordinance: federal rules already require it (see Why).
- *Why:* Federal rules require the City's communications to work for people with disabilities (28 CFR 35.160), and its websites and apps to meet WCAG 2.1 AA from April 26, 2027 (28 CFR 35.200).

**Who is affected**

- **Everyone:** one official to call, a published winter plan with free sand or de-icer and a clearing map, the end of every snow emergency announced, and a guide each November.
- **City staff:** one lead official coordinating departments; the winter plan, GPS on machines and a public map, sand and de-icer; the notices, a mailing, 311 close codes, and a yearly report with costs and its data; the ADA Coordinator answers urgent blocked-route requests within 12 hours.

### Sec. 12-2: water and ice from buildings

Water from a building freezes on the sidewalk on sunny days, not only during storms. Name the sources, require the fix, and give the rule a ticket.

**Title**

- *Today:* "Water run-off and washing cars"
- *Proposed:* Keep "Water run-off and washing cars".
- *Why:* Icicles and roof snow move to Sec. 12-8(c)(6), with the other sidewalk duties, so the title stays.

**What's banned**

- *Today:* (a) "No person shall suffer his or her building or awning or other projection thereon to shed water on any sidewalk or street to the injury or inconvenience of travelers."
- *Proposed:* "No building, awning or other projection may shed or discharge water onto a sidewalk, curb ramp or bus stop. This includes roofs, downspouts, sump pumps and appliances. Water must be piped under the sidewalk to the gutter, or onto the owner's own ground."
- *Why:* "To the injury or inconvenience of travelers" invites argument after someone has already fallen. Naming the sources tells owners what to check. Auburn's bylaw (2017 text) names sump pumps and drains; Cambridge bans roof leaders that send water across the sidewalk (Municipal Code 12.16.070), and Madison bans downspouts that empty onto the sidewalk (General Ordinances 10.29). Like today's rule, it applies all year: an air conditioner dripping onto the sidewalk in July is covered too.

**Other discharge**

- *Today:* (b) bans water from washing cars "or for other purposes" flowing "upon or across the surface of any sidewalk".
- *Proposed:* Keep.
- *Why:* Already covers hoses and washing.

**Ice from a building**

- *Today:* None. Sec. 12-8 applies only after a weather event.
- *Proposed:* No separate rule: "clear" covers ice "whatever the source", roofs included (Sec. 12-8(a)), and the owner must keep it clear (Sec. 12-8(c)).
- *Why:* Downspout ice forms on sunny days after the storm. Sec. 12-8 already makes the owner clear it, and (a) here requires the fix at the source.

**Fines**

- *Today:* Sec. 12-2 has no row in the Sec. 1-11(b) fine schedule.
- *Proposed:* Sec. 12-2 joins the Sec. 12-8 row of the fine schedule in Section 1-11(b).
- *Why:* Without a row in the fine schedule, inspectors have no ticket to write for a downspout icing the sidewalk.

**Who is affected**

- **Owners:** a one-time fix for downspouts or sump pumps that drain onto the sidewalk.
- **City staff:** a new ticket type.

### Sec. 12-9, repealed (now Sec. 12-8(e) and (h)): snow into streets, bike lanes and bus stops

Repealed. Its bans join Sec. 12-8(e), one list of where snow and objects may not go; the rule for spreading snow in the street becomes 12-8(e)(2), with a temperature test anyone can check; and DPW's power to remove snow placed where it may not go joins 12-8(h).

**Title**

- *Today:* "Shoveling snow on streets"
- *Proposed:* Repeal the section; its rules move to Sec. 12-8.
- *Why:* One list of where snow may not go, in the section owners already read, replaces two.

**Who**

- *Today:* "No owner, tenant or occupant of land or a building, or any agent thereof"
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(e)(1): "No one, contractors included".
- *Why:* Contractors clear many lots and driveways; naming them ends any debate over who is an "agent".

**Where**

- *Today:* "in any public way"
- *Proposed:* Sec. 12-8(e)(1) bans snow on any sidewalk, curb ramp, crosswalk, bus stop or bike lane, and "on a street except as paragraph (2) allows"; (e)(2) covers snow "from private land, or from the sidewalk, curb ramp or driveway apron abutting it".
- *Why:* Snow pushed into a bike lane or bus stop stays there long after the street is plowed, because no one is assigned to clear it. Madison bans putting private snow on any sidewalk, street or bike path, and the City removes it at the owner's cost (General Ordinances 10.23). The sidewalk is public land, so without the added words snow shoveled off it isn't "from private land"; Madison's code closes the same gap by counting the abutting sidewalk, terrace and driveway apron as part of the property (10.23(2)(a)).

**Exception**

- *Today:* "unless said snow or ice is broken up and spread evenly, to a thickness of no more than three inches, during daylight hours, when the mean temperature for that day is above 40 degrees Fahrenheit"
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(e)(2): "Snow or ice from private land, or from the sidewalk, curb ramp or driveway apron abutting it, may go on a street only if broken up and spread no more than 3 inches thick, between 8 a.m. and 7 p.m., above 40°F."
- *Why:* A day's mean temperature is known only after the day ends, so no one can follow or enforce the current test. Fixed hours, 8 a.m. to 7 p.m., are the same every day, where "daylight" moves with the season.

**Fines**

- *Today:* "(b) ... fined in accordance with section 1-11"
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(h): "The Commissioner may also remove snow or ice placed in violation of subsection (e) and charge the owner a fee the City sets to match its cost. Tickets, City clearing and removal do not bar each other." The ticket goes to the owner of the property the snow or ice came from (Sec. 1-11(d)(1)).
- *Why:* One name to find, as in Sec. 12-8. The owner recovers from a careless contractor by contract. Madison removes illegally placed snow and charges the owner (General Ordinances 10.23(3)).

**Who is affected**

- **Owners:** pay the ticket if their contractor pushes snow into the street, a bike lane or a bus stop.
- **City staff:** a temperature test they can check on the spot.

### Sec. 12-18: space savers and objects in the path

The City treats space savers as illegal under a section titled "at night" that never names them. Say it outright, and keep bins and other objects out of the cleared path.

**Title**

- *Today:* "Obstacles in streets or sidewalks at night" (fine schedule: "overnight")
- *Proposed:* "Obstacles in streets and sidewalks"
- *Why:* The text already says "at any time"; the title invites the argument that daytime is fine.

**Space savers**

- *Today:* Not named. (a) bans "any vehicle or any other obstacle or article on any sidewalk or in any public or private way ... without first obtaining a permit".
- *Proposed:* "(e) No one may put a chair, cone or any other object in a street to hold a parking space. The City may remove and discard it at once. Each object is a separate offense."
- *Why:* The City's snow page already says it prohibits space savers and may collect and dispose of them; residents should find that in the code. Boston, by contrast, allows them for 48 hours after a snow emergency, except in the South End and Bay Village.

**Objects in the path**

- *Today:* Covered only in general by (a).
- *Proposed:* In Sec. 12-8(e)(4): "No object, including a trash or recycling bin, may stand anywhere subsection (c) requires clearing or in a bus stop. Before each collection, the owner must clear a spot for its bins at the curb that collectors can reach from the street." The ticket goes to the owner of the property the object stands in front of (Sec. 1-11(d)(1)).
- *Why:* Bins left in the cleared strip push wheelchairs into the street. The ticket goes to the owner, as for the sidewalk itself, so no one has to find out whose bin it is. Boston and Brookline ask for bins at the street edge, not behind snowbanks, so crews can collect them; this rule also keeps them out of the walking path.

**Fine**

- *Today:* $100 per offense; "each day the violation exists shall constitute a separate and new violation"
- *Proposed:* Keep.
- *Why:* Works as is.

**Who is affected**

- **Everyone:** no saved parking spots; bins out of the path, with a spot cleared at the curb for pickup.
- **City staff:** the City already removes space savers; bin tickets go to the owner of record, like sidewalk tickets.

### Sec. 1-11: fines, inspections and follow-up

Make the ticket automatic and the amount simple: inspectors must ticket every violation they find, two fine levels read from City records, and a flat clearing fee on top when City crews do the work. State law caps a ticket at $300, so pressure comes from a new ticket each day and the clearing fee, not bigger fines.

**Fine amounts**

- *Today:* 12-8 and 12-9: 1–3 units $100 / $200 / $300; 4+ units and businesses $150 / $300. Counted per property, Oct 1 to May 1.
- *Proposed:* "$150 for a building of 1 to 3 units where at least one unit has the residential exemption; $300 for any other property, and for any property for ice not leveled and covered with sand or de-icer by its deadline, or while a construction permit under Section 12-8(g) is open." (one row in Section 1-11(b) for Sec. 12-2 and 12-8)
- *Why:* Two amounts, both read from City records in seconds, and no offense history to look up (today's 1st/2nd/3rd count runs per property from Oct 1 to May 1). PTAC's March 2025 letter asked for higher fines for ice, landlords and developers; $300 is the state cap. Repeat failures still add up: a new ticket each day, plus the clearing fee. The line follows today's split between 1–3 unit and larger buildings, and the residential exemption shows whether an owner lives there.

**Counting period**

- *Today:* (a) "except that snow removal violations shall be counted from October 1 through May 1 of the following year"
- *Proposed:* Strike it.
- *Why:* With two flat fines there is no 1st, 2nd or 3rd offense to count. The 3-ticket license rule in Sec. 1-11(d)(6) still counts from October 1 to May 1.

**Construction sites**

- *Today:* Same fines as any property of its type.
- *Proposed:* In the Section 1-11(b) row, "$300 … for any property for ice not leveled and covered with sand or de-icer by its deadline, or while a construction permit under Section 12-8(g) is open". Those permits cover dumpsters, sidewalk obstructions and openings, building and demolition.
- *Why:* Sites have crews and equipment, so a missed deadline is a choice. Even an owner-occupied home pays $300 while a permit is open, and the permit is easy to check: it is open or it isn't. The ice test follows Boston's code, where ice passes if it is "made level and completely covered with sand" (CBC 16-12.16(B)). The bigger lever is the permit itself (see construction sites under Sec. 12-8).

**Who gets the ticket**

- *Today:* Not stated; any "person ... in charge" can be fined.
- *Proposed:* "A ticket under Section 12-2 or 12-8 names the owner of the property the sidewalk abuts, the snow or ice came from, or the object stands in front of. If not handed over, it is mailed within 15 days to the tax-bill address, or to a condominium's trustees or managing agent."
- *Why:* One name, found in seconds: no investigation, no appeals over the wrong person, and absentee owners learn of the problem.

**Duty to ticket**

- *Today:* Not in the code (in practice, "the citations range from a warning to up to $300", CBS Boston, Feb 19, 2025).
- *Proposed:* "An inspector who finds a violation must issue a ticket."
- *Why:* Takes the decision off inspectors and the administration: when neighbors push back, the answer is the ordinance, not someone's discretion. Exceptions are settled in advance, by registration, not on the sidewalk. Madison tells residents it "will not issue warnings before writing a citation" (City of Madison, Nov 2025). The state's Office on Disability calls it "critical for municipalities to enforce snow removal responsibilities with ticketing" (2025).

**Where inspectors look**

- *Today:* Not stated.
- *Proposed:* "During and after each snowfall of 1 inch or more at Boston Logan Airport, and within 24 hours after each snow emergency's deadline, inspectors must check the priority routes, whether or not anyone complained."
- *Why:* Complaints follow who reports: in 2025/26, Ward 1 filed 1,010 sidewalk reports and Ward 7 filed 142. Blocks where no one calls get no inspection today. Every storm that gets a notice counts, small ones included: residents tell PTAC that small storms leave the worst ice.

**Repeat tickets**

- *Today:* (a) "Each day a violation continues shall constitute a separate offense."
- *Proposed:* Keep, tied to the re-inspection in Sec. 12-8(h).
- *Why:* Makes the daily offense real. Cambridge ($50) and Worcester ($75) fine by the day.

**Language**

- *Today:* Not stated.
- *Proposed:* "Tickets and notices include how to pay, appeal and get help, in the languages in which the City publishes its snow rules."
- *Why:* The City already publishes its snow rules in several languages; the ticket should match.

**Where the money goes**

- *Today:* Not stated.
- *Proposed:* "Fines and clearing charges under Section 12-8 must fund the snow assistance program and City clearing of curb ramps and bus stops, subject to appropriation."
- *Why:* Ties enforcement to help. Fines normally go to the City's general fund, so this needs a dedicated fund set up by the Council and Mayor; the Law Department should advise on the form.

**Unpaid fines**

- *Today:* (c) may block local licenses and permits, or become a municipal charges lien.
- *Proposed:* Keep.
- *Why:* Works as is.

**Business licenses**

- *Today:* Only unpaid fines can touch a license (Sec. 1-11(c)).
- *Proposed:* "If 3 or more Section 12-8 tickets are issued between October 1 and May 1 for a property where a business holds a City license or permit, the issuing authority may suspend or refuse to renew it, after notice and a hearing."
- *Why:* A shop or parking lot that depends on a City license has every reason to hire a reliable contractor. Minneapolis makes failure to clear grounds to revoke the City license of a business whose licensed operation needs a driveway or curb cut (Code 445.40). The 3-ticket threshold and the hearing keep one bad storm from costing anyone a license.

**Who is affected**

- **Homeowners:** $150 instead of $100 for a first ticket, and no warning on a first miss.
- **Owners who don't live in their 1- to 3-unit building, larger buildings, businesses and construction sites:** $300 instead of $100 or $150 for a first ticket.
- **City staff:** no judgment calls on the sidewalk and no 1st/2nd/3rd offense ladder to track, but more tickets to process and more inspector hours, since every storm counts, small ones included.

## Attachment B: Beyond the ordinance

A few limits sit in state law or with other agencies. The Council can't change them alone, but it can ask.

## Attachment C: State law check

PTAC wrote these rules for Somerville, not to copy the state or other cities. For each proposal we asked one question: could a state law or the State Constitution stop the City from doing it? Where the answer was yes or maybe, we changed the wording to keep the goal and stay within the City's powers. This is PTAC's reading of the law, not legal advice; the Law Department should confirm it before the Council votes.

**How courts decide.** A city ordinance stands unless it is in "a sharp conflict" with a state law: the Legislature clearly meant to leave no room for local rules, or the state law's purpose can't be met alongside the local one (Bloom v. Worcester, 1973). The State Constitution adds one limit: a city may not "enact private or civil law governing civil relationships except as an incident to an exercise of an independent municipal power" (Home Rule Amendment, art. 89 §7(5)). Leases fall under that limit: "An ordinance which affects the landlord-tenant relationship is a 'private or civil law governing civil relationships'" (Bannerman v. Fall River, 1984).

| Proposal | What state law says | Our reading | What we did |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Tickets go only to the owner of record (Sec. 12-8(b), 1-11) | c. 40 §21(3) lets cities require sidewalk snow removal, and the penalty "shall apply to the owner of abutting property or his agent having charge thereof". c. 85 §5, which today's Sec. 12-8 also cites, says "owner or occupant" but caps a city's fine at $50. | Supported. Owner-only fits the $300 statute better than today's text, which tickets tenants. | Kept. The proposed text now cites c. 40 §21, not c. 85 §5. |
| Condos: the association gets the ticket | c. 183A §14 lets a city charge "all other assessments, … rates and charges of every nature" to the organization of unit owners, with any lien split among the units by their percentages. §10 makes it "subject to suit" over the common areas, which include the land. | Supported: the association is the owners' agent in charge of the sidewalk. | Kept. An unpaid fee becomes a lien split across the units. |
| $150 and $300 tickets | c. 40 §21 caps penalties at $300 per offense, and c. 40 §21D caps a ticket at $300. c. 40U §5, if the City used it for snow, caps tickets at $200. c. 59 §5C grants the residential exemption per parcel, condo units included. | Within the cap under §21D. The $150 test is a lookup in assessing records. | Kept. Tickets go under §21D. |
| Tickets by mail | c. 40 §21D: hand the ticket over "if possible"; otherwise mail it "to the offender's last known address, within fifteen days after said violation". | Works: under the ordinance, the owner is the offender. | Added "within 15 days" to Sec. 1-11. |
| "No lease may make a tenant shovel or pay" (an approach we considered and dropped) | Leases are civil law under art. 89 §7(5) (Marshal House v. Rent Review Board, 1970; Bannerman). The State Sanitary Code lets a written lease shift snow duty only for exits under the tenant's "exclusive use and control" (105 CMR 410.260(D)(4)); it doesn't reach the public sidewalk. | The riskiest line in the draft: it rewrote lease terms. Ticketing only the owner gets the same result for enforcement without touching the lease. | Rewritten: the duty stays with the owner for enforcement, whatever a lease says. Attachment B asks the state to protect tenants in the lease itself. |
| Ramps, crosswalk openings, bus stops, hydrants, the planted strip (Sec. 12-8(c), (e)) | c. 40 §21(3) speaks of "sidewalks, within the limits of the public ways"; no statute addresses the street edge, for or against. c. 85 §7B fines pushing snow onto a state highway "so as to impede the flow of traffic". | Within the City's general home rule power (art. 89 §6), since no statute conflicts, but untested: the least certain part of the draft. Snow from a cut goes onto the owner's land or the planted strip, and into the road only as Sec. 12-8(e)(2) allows. | Kept. The first question for the Law Department. |
| Flat clearing fee and lien (Sec. 12-8(h)) | A fee must pay for a service and be reasonable and roughly proportional to its cost (Emerson College v. Boston, 1984; Silva v. Attleboro, 2009). c. 85 §6 lets a city that accepts it charge abutters "the whole or any part of the cost" of clearing their sidewalks. Liens: Somerville's own Acts of 1996, c. 252 (cited in Sec. 1-11(c)), or c. 40 §58, with a council vote for each type of charge. | Valid as written: charged only where City crews cleared, at average cost. | Kept. |
| Construction: holding inspections (an approach we considered and dropped) | The State Building Code is "intended to occupy the field of building regulation" (St. George Greek Orthodox Cathedral v. Fire Department of Springfield, 2012). c. 40 §57 lets a city that accepts it deny or suspend a building permit for unpaid fines, after notice, a hearing and an offer of a payment plan. | Holding building inspections over tickets risks preemption. Dumpster, obstruction and sidewalk-opening permits are the City's own (Sec. 12-12, 12-20, 12-77). | Rewritten: after 3 tickets the City may suspend the sidewalk permit; unpaid tickets reach the building permit only through Sec. 1-11(c), with notice and a hearing. |
| Fines for the assistance program (Sec. 1-11) | Fines "enure to the city or town for such use as said city or town may direct" (c. 40 §21D), but every receipt goes to the treasury and needs an appropriation (c. 44 §53). c. 40 §5B, if accepted, lets a city dedicate a receipt to a fund without appropriation. | "Subject to appropriation", as drafted, is allowed. Skipping appropriation would need §5B, if the state's Division of Local Services agrees fines qualify. | Kept. |
| City deadlines: "must clear" (Sec. 12-8(h), 12-8A) | A city "shall not be liable" for injury from snow or ice on a public way that is otherwise reasonably safe (c. 84 §17). The Tort Claims Act shields discretionary choices (c. 258 §10(b)); a fixed duty has less of that cover. | §17 still applies; firm deadlines may narrow one defense. | Added Sec. 12-8(j): the duties are enforced by the City and create no new right to sue the City or an owner. |
| Water from buildings (Sec. 12-2(a)) | The MWRA bars groundwater and roof runoff from its sewers (360 CMR 10.023). | "Piped to a drain" could send sump water into a sewer. | Rewritten: piped to the gutter or onto the owner's own ground. |
| Roof snow and ice (Sec. 12-8(c)(6)) | c. 40 §21(2) and (4) let cities require owners to prevent and remove snow and ice falling from roofs. | Expressly allowed. | Kept. |
| Business licenses (new Sec. 1-11(d)(6)) | c. 40 §57 covers licenses only for unpaid charges. Suspending a license for repeated violations rests on the power that grants each license: the City's own ordinances, or the state law behind licenses such as liquor licenses. | Likely allowed for licenses and permits the City grants under its own ordinances; less certain for state-defined licenses. | Added, with a 3-ticket threshold, notice and a hearing. For the Law Department to confirm which licenses it reaches. |
| Everything else: deadline, width, driveways, clearing while it snows, notices, data, winter plan, space savers, bins, accessible parking, City duties | No conflicting statute found. c. 40U §5, where a city uses it, asks only that a snow ordinance be "specific as to the width of the area to be cleared and the standards for clearance". | Within the City's power. | Kept. |

**For the Law Department.** Confirm which local-option statutes the City has accepted: c. 40 §57 (permits and unpaid fines), c. 40 §5B (dedicated funds) and c. 85 §6 (clearing charges); which lien power the clearing fee uses; and whether the duties at the street edge rest on general home rule power, since no statute speaks to them.
